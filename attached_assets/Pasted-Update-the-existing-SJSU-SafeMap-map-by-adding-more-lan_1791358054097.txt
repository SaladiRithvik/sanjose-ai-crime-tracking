4. Landmark design

- Keep the current muted grayscale basemap and regal report-volume color scheme unchanged.
- Make landmarks visually distinct from report markers using a neutral icon or outline style.
- Use small, clean icons:
  - Parking icon for garages
  - Building icon for campus buildings
  - Food or fork-and-knife icon for dining areas
- Do not use red, gold, orange, or burgundy for landmarks because those colors are reserved for report-volume visualization.
- Keep landmark labels readable but subtle.
- Do not make landmarks larger or more visually dominant than safety-report markers.
- Avoid overlapping labels by slightly offsetting them or hiding lower-priority labels when zoomed out.

5. Interaction

- Landmarks should be clickable.
- Clicking a landmark may show a small, clean information popup with its name and relevant details.
- Parking garage popups may show address, number of levels, capacity, and hours.
- Campus-building popups may show the building name.
- Dining-area popups may show the area name and general description.
- Do not show a large dropdown or oversized scrollable panel.
- Do not confuse landmark clicks with report-marker clicks.
- Clicking a report cluster must continue to immediately zoom into the cluster as it currently should.

6. Map organization

- Keep safety reports as the primary map layer.
- Add a simple landmark visibility control or layer toggle if the map already supports layer controls.
- Landmarks should be visible by default for context but should be easy to hide if the map becomes crowded.
- Ensure landmarks appear correctly in both the SJSU Campus and Downtown views.
- Ensure the layout works on desktop and mobile.

- Landmarks do not affect the approved-report total.

The goal is to add useful geographic context so users can understand where reports occur relative to parking garages, campus buildings, and nearby dining areas without changing the current visual design or safety-report workflow.
